# Oracle Cloud Leads Free Godot CI

Oracle Cloud's Always Free tier is the only permanent free VPS with enough RAM and CPU to run Godot builds, offering 2 ARM OCPUs and 12 GB of memory at no cost, while AWS, GCP, and Azure free VMs top out at 1 GiB of RAM and are time-limited or credit-burstable. For most developers, though, managed free CI minutes — especially GitHub Actions' 2,000 free minutes per month for private repos and unlimited minutes for public repos — or a self-hosted runner on hardware already owned remain the most practical free paths, because even Oracle's free VM requires ARM-specific setup and carries capacity and idle-reclamation risks. The binding constraint across every free option is not capability — Godot exports run headlessly on any Linux host with the barichello/godot-ci Docker image or a direct binary install — but RAM, CPU, and minutes.

## Oracle Cloud Always Free is the only free VPS viable for Godot

Oracle's Always Free compute provides a shared pool of 2 OCPUs and 12 GB of RAM on Ampere A1 ARM, configurable as one instance or split across up to four, plus up to two AMD E2.1.Micro instances at 1/8 OCPU bursting to 1 OCPU with 1 GB RAM each, backed by 200 GB of pooled block storage and 10 TB of monthly egress ([Oracle Always Free Resources](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)). These limits were halved from 4 OCPUs and 24 GB on June 15, 2026, a change Oracle did not formally announce, so older tutorials still cite the larger allocation ([askgpu.com](https://askgpu.com/updates/oracle-cloud-halves-always-free-arm-compute/)). For Godot specifically, the A1 shape is the only viable one: Godot ships ARM64 Linux binaries and official ARM64 export templates since Godot 4.1+, and Oracle even publishes an official blog and quickstart repo for running GitHub Actions runners on its Arm platform ([Oracle blog](https://blogs.oracle.com/cloud-infrastructure/announcing-github-actions-arm-runners-for-the-arm-compute-platform-on-oracle-cloud-infrastructure); [GitHub](https://github.com/oracle-quickstart/oci-github-actions-runner)). A practical setup is a single 2-OCPU/12 GB A1 instance running Ubuntu or Oracle Linux, with Godot installed from its official Linux ARM64 binary plus export templates, registered as a self-hosted GitHub Actions runner. The catch is architecture: the host is ARM64, so cross-compiling to x86_64, Windows, Web, or Android needs the respective toolchains (MinGW, Emscripten, Android NDK), which run fine on ARM64 hosts but add setup work. The AMD micro instances' 1 GB RAM and 1/8 OCPU baseline cannot run Godot builds at all. The operational caveats are significant: capacity errors ("out of host capacity") are common and require retrying in another availability domain or waiting; idle reclamation triggers after 7 days when CPU, network, and (on A1) memory 95th percentiles all stay below 20%, so a CI runner needs scheduled builds or a keep-alive daemon; accounts idle 30 days may be suspended; credit or debit card verification requires a $1 hold and rejects prepaid or virtual cards; the home region chosen at signup is permanent; and there is no SLA or Oracle Support for Always Free-only accounts ([Oracle Cloud Free Tier FAQ](https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/faq/)).

## AWS, GCP, and Azure free VMs are too small and temporary for Godot

AWS offers no permanent free EC2 instance. Accounts created before July 15, 2025 keep a legacy 12-month offer of 750 hours per month of t2.micro or t3.micro (1 vCPU / 1 GiB RAM), and accounts created on or after that date receive a credit-based Free Plan of $100 plus up to $100 in earned credits that expires in 6 months ([AWS Free Tier](https://www.aws.amazon.com/free); [AWS News Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-free-tier-update-new-customers-can-get-started-and-explore-aws-with-up-to-200-in-credits)). GCP is the only major hyperscaler with a permanent free VM: one non-preemptible e2-micro with 2 shared vCPUs, 1 GiB RAM, and only 0.25 vCPU sustained, plus 30 GB of standard persistent disk and 1 GB of egress per month, available only in us-west1, us-central1, or us-east1, alongside a 90-day $300 trial ([GCP free cloud features](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)). Azure provides 750 hours per month of burstable B-series VMs (B1s, B2pts v2, B2ats v2) free for 12 months, but the B1s SKU is being retired in many regions and can show "NotAvailableForSubscription" on Free Trial subscriptions, leaving the 2-vCPU/1-GiB B2ats v2 or B2pts v2 as the safer free options ([Azure free services docs](http://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/manage/create-free-services); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/answers/questions/5975585/b1s-b2ats-v2-vm-sizes-show-notavailableforsubscrip)). Godot 4's documented minimum for the native editor is 3-6 GB RAM, and while headless export and test runs need far less — community reports suggest 1-2 GB — a 1 GiB VM is still the binding constraint, and CPU-credit bursting on GCP's e2-micro and Azure's B1s will throttle sustained builds ([Godot system requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/system_requirements.html)). The one genuinely useful hyperscaler CI option is GCP's 2,500 free Cloud Build build-minutes per month on e2-standard-2 machines with 2 vCPUs and 8 GB RAM, which is a stronger fit for Godot than any free VM ([GCP free cloud features](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)).

## Render, Railway, and Fly.io are not practical CI hosts

Fly.io retired its free allowances for new accounts on October 7, 2024; new organizations start on usage-based billing with a $5 per month minimum, and the only free access is a 7-day or 2-VM-hour trial capped at 10 machines, 4 GB RAM, and 2 vCPUs, with no GPUs and no dedicated IPv4 ([Fly.io Pricing 2026](https://temps.sh/blog/fly-io-pricing-2026); [Fly.io Free Tier 2026](https://www.saaspricepulse.com/blog/flyio-free-tier-2026)). Render has a genuine permanent free tier — a 512 MB RAM, 0.1 CPU web service that sleeps after 15 minutes of inactivity, 750 instance hours per month, plus a free PostgreSQL instance that expires after 30 days — but 512 MB RAM and 0.1 CPU is below what Godot exports need, and the 15-minute sleep makes it unsuitable as a responsive CI runner; Render Workflows can run Godot builds on flex compute up to 1 CPU and 4 GB RAM, but that is billed per use, not free ([Render Pricing](https://render.com/pricing); [Render Workflows Limits](https://render.com/docs/workflows-limits)). Railway's Free plan provides only $1 per month of non-rollover usage credit with 1 vCPU and 0.5 GB RAM per service, following a one-time $5 trial credit for new accounts, so it cannot run Godot builds either ([Railway Free Tier 2026](https://medium.com/@kuberns/railway-free-tier-in-2026-what-you-get-and-when-it-runs-out-2101fdca0998)). None of these three platforms are viable as free Godot CI hosts; Render and Railway at most suit hosting deployed game builds as static sites.

## Managed CI minutes and self-hosted runners are the practical free paths

GitHub Actions gives 2,000 free minutes per month for private repositories and unlimited minutes for public repositories on standard Linux runners with 2 vCPUs, 8 GB RAM, and 14 GB SSD, and self-hosted runners carry no per-minute charge from GitHub, though since March 2026 private repos incur a $0.002 per minute platform fee ([GitHub Docs: GitHub-hosted runners reference](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners); [Top 5 CI/CD Platforms 2026](https://guptadeepak.com/tools/top-5-ci-cd-platforms-2026)). GitLab CI's Free tier is far tighter at 400 compute minutes per month per group, and CircleCI's Free plan offers 30,000 credits per month — roughly 3,000 minutes on Medium Linux — but free-plan users cannot purchase additional credits ([GitLab Pricing](https://about.gitlab.com/pricing); [CircleCI Pricing 2026](https://cicdcost.com/circleci-pricing)). Godot builds run on all of these via the barichello/godot-ci Docker image or GameCI's Godot support, and a typical Godot export takes 5-15 minutes depending on project size and target platform, so GitHub's 2,000 free minutes exhaust in roughly two to three weeks of daily builds on a private repo ([GitHub Marketplace: godot-ci](https://github.com/marketplace/actions/godot-ci); [GameCI FAQ](https://game.ci/docs/faq)). The practical recommendation for a developer who already owns a Mac Mini, PC, or home server is a self-hosted runner: registration is a one-time config.sh step with a one-hour token, the runner needs only outbound HTTPS on port 443 and roughly 70 kbps bandwidth, and Godot can be invoked directly as `godot --headless --export-release "Preset" build/output` or through the chickensoft-games/setup-godot action on macOS and the barichello/godot-ci container on Linux ([GitHub Docs: Adding self-hosted runners](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/hosting-your-own-runners/managing-self-hosted-runners/adding-self-hosted-runners); [Godot Docs: Command line tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html)). The important caveats are that one runner handles only one job at a time regardless of core count, runners are stateful processes rather than clean VMs so filesystem side-effects persist across jobs, and GitHub strongly discourages self-hosted runners for public repositories because fork pull requests can run arbitrary code on the machine ([GitHub Docs: Secure use reference](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/security/secure-use)).

## Conclusion

The right free Godot CI choice depends on what you already have and what you are willing to operate. If you own any always-on machine, a self-hosted GitHub Actions runner is the best free option — unlimited minutes, 8 GB-plus of RAM, and no capacity or reclamation risk — and it is the path Oracle's own documentation supports for its Arm platform. If you do not own hardware and cannot pay, Oracle Cloud Always Free is the only free VPS with enough RAM and CPU to run Godot builds, but you accept ARM setup, capacity errors, and a 7-day idle-reclamation window that demands active monitoring. If neither appeals, GitHub Actions' 2,000 free minutes per month on standard 8 GB runners is the most capable managed option and is the right starting point for private projects, with public repositories getting unlimited minutes. The 1 GiB free VMs from GCP and Azure and the PaaS free tiers from Render, Railway, and Fly.io should be treated as non-starters for Godot builds rather than as cheaper alternatives.
